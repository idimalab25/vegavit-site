lambit2FA Privacy Policy

Effective date: 17 August 2026
Last updated: 14 September 2026

In short

lambit2FA collects no data at all. Neither personal nor anonymised, no analytics, no crash reports.

Everything you add to the app stays on your device. If you turn synchronisation on, your devices exchange records through our server — encrypted in such a way that only you can read them. Until you turn it on, the app does not contact our server at all.

The app goes online for two things: a clock check, no more than once a day (section 5), and synchronisation, if you have turned it on (section 6).

2. What data you enter into the app

To generate verification codes, the app stores:

In readable form this data never leaves the device. We do not receive it, do not see it and cannot restore it. If synchronisation is on, it goes to our server encrypted — with a key we do not have (section 6).

3. Where it is stored

  • iOS and macOS: in the system Keychain with the access level “after first unlock, this device only”. It follows that the data does not go into the iCloud backup and is not transferred to a new device automatically.
  • Android: in a local database encrypted with AES-256. The encryption key is held in the hardware-backed Android Keystore. Automatic upload to Google Drive is disabled (allowBackup=false).

4. What the app does NOT do

  • it does not send your codes and keys anywhere in readable form, including to us;
  • it does not contact third-party services, apart from the clock check (section 5);
  • it does not contact our server until you have turned synchronisation on;
  • it does not use analytics, counters, advertising or tracking libraries;
  • it does not collect crash reports;
  • it does not read your address book, location, calendar, photos or call history;
  • it does not show advertising;
  • it does not share or sell data to third parties — there is nothing to share.

5. Going online: the clock check

A code is computed from the current time. A clock that has drifted produces codes that do not work, and the person concludes that the app is broken. That is why, occasionally — no more than once a day — the app asks several large websites (apple.com, google.com, duckduckgo.com, wikipedia.org, cloudflare.com) for the exact time and reads only the timestamp from the reply.

What leaves the device: the fact of the request itself, that is your network address, just as when opening any web page. No codes, no keys, no service names and no device identifiers are transmitted — the request contains nothing except the request.

Why these addresses: the phone contacts them constantly anyway, without us. Contacting them tells nothing new about you.

The correction can be switched off: “Settings” → “Time” → “Do not apply correction”.

6. Synchronisation between devices

Synchronisation is off until you turn it on. While it is off, the app does not contact our server at all.

If you have turned it on:

  • a record goes to the server, encrypted on your device with a key that is created when you link your own devices to each other. The key stays on them and is not transmitted to us;
  • the server sees only a set of bytes and a mailbox number computed from your key. It receives no name, no email and no device identifier — we do not have them;
  • the network address a request came from is not recorded. The request log is switched off deliberately. The address is needed by the network to deliver the reply and exists exactly as long as the request lasts;
  • records expire by themselves: those older than 400 days are no longer served and are deleted by the server.

There is no deletion on request — neither for you nor for us. The mailbox stops being filled when you switch synchronisation off, and expires by time. The cloud is not a replacement for a backup: it holds what your devices exchange, not an archive for the future.

7. Device permissions

Camera — requested only when you press “Scan” yourself. It is used to recognise a QR code. The image is processed in memory and is neither saved nor transmitted anywhere. You may withhold the permission: the secret key can be typed in by hand.

Face ID, Touch ID, fingerprint — only to unlock the app. The check is performed by the operating system; the app receives only a “yes” or “no” answer and never has access to the biometric samples.

File access — only to the files you have selected yourself in the system dialog, and only to read or write a backup.

8. Backups

You create a backup yourself and decide yourself where to send it.

The file is encrypted on the device before it leaves it: AES-256-CBC, the key is derived from your password with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 and 10 000 iterations, integrity is confirmed with HMAC-SHA256. The password is stored nowhere — neither on the device nor with us.

A forgotten password means the backup is lost for good. This is not a shortcoming but a direct consequence of the encryption being real: there is no way around it, neither for you nor for us.

If you send the backup by email or through a messenger, the rules of the service you used apply to the transferred file. We have no influence over them.

9. Children

The app is not intended for children and collects no data about anyone, children included. The age rating is set because of the nature of the task, not because of the content.

10. Your rights

Secret keys, service names and settings are held on your device. We do not have them, therefore requesting access to them from us, their correction or deletion is not needed and is technically impossible.

You can delete everything on the device yourself and instantly: “Settings” → “Delete all accounts”, or by removing the app from the device.

If synchronisation is on, encrypted records from your devices are held on our server. We cannot read them, and there is nothing to link them to a person with: we receive no name, no email and no device identifier, and we do not record network addresses. There is no deletion of these records on request, neither for you nor for us (section 6); they expire by time. Switch synchronisation off and the mailbox will stop being filled.

The rights under GDPR (EU), CCPA (California) and Federal Law 152-FZ (Russia) are honoured by the very way the matter is arranged: data by which a person could be identified does not arise on our side.
